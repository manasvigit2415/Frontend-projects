Field Photography Tips

Hard-won wisdom from hundreds of days in the field, distilled into practical guidance for every level.

🌅

Chase the Golden Hour

The hour after sunrise and before sunset delivers the softest, most directional light. Animals are also most active during these cooler periods.

🎯

Use the Rule of Thirds

Place your subject on an intersection point rather than centred. This creates dynamic tension and draws the eye naturally through the frame.

🤫

Patience Beats Equipment

The best wildlife photographs come from spending hours at the same location, letting animals forget you're there. A budget camera and infinite patience beats an expensive one with none.

📐

Get to Eye Level

Photographing animals from their own eye level creates intimacy and removes the hierarchical distance of shooting from above. Lie in the mud if you must.

🌿

Use Natural Framing

Branches, leaves, and rock formations can frame your subject and add depth. These foreground elements also mask the feeling of a snapshot and add environmental context.

🔇

Leave No Trace

The photograph is less important than the animal's wellbeing. Never bait, call-in, or stress wildlife for a shot. A photograph taken ethically is worth ten taken at cost.
